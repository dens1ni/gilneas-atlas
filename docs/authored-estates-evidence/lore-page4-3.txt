**Harvest-witches**[2] (or **harvest witches**)[3] are [humans](/wiki/Human) gifted with minor [control](/wiki/Druid) over [nature](/wiki/Nature), a practice they refer to as the **old ways**.[1][4] They are not widespread, and live on the fringes of [Gilnean](/wiki/Gilneas_(kingdom)) society.

## Background

Prior to the founding of the [Empire of Arathor](/wiki/Arathor), many [human tribes](/wiki/Early_humans) had primitive belief systems that incorporated simple [nature](/wiki/Nature)[magic](/wiki/Magic),[3] mainly crude forms of [druidism](/wiki/Druid) and [shamanism](/wiki/Shaman).[5] However, the rise of organized [religion](/wiki/Religion) such as the [Holy Light](/wiki/Light) and the [arcane](/wiki/Arcane) magics [introduced](/wiki/One_Hundred) by the [high elves](/wiki/High_elf) quickly supplanted such traditions. The [kingdom of Gilneas](/wiki/Gilneas_(kingdom)), due to its relative isolation, retained a degree of their ancient culture into the contemporary era. The religious leaders of what was in Gilneas referred to as the "old ways" eventually became "harvest-witches", those who used their nature powers to augment Gilneas' agricultural output during and following its period of industrialization.[3]

Due to the presence of harvest-witches in their culture, when Gilneans learned about [night elf](/wiki/Night_elf) druids (albeit through second, third, and even fourth-hand sources) they became fascinated by them and their exotic connotations, to the point where many[3]—including the witches themselves[4]—started referring to harvest-witches as "druids". This was quite far from the truth, however, as few Gilneans had any idea what a druid actually was. Harvest-witches have a limited control over nature, especially plant life, and the powers of harvest-witches bear a coincidental resemblance to the low-level abilities of actual druids.[3]

At one point after Gilneas cut itself off from the outside world with the [Greymane Wall](/wiki/Greymane_Wall), the kingdom was struck by a famine that drove its inhabitants to the edge of extinction. The harvest-witches, keepers of the old ways, called upon the earth's blessings and restored the harvest, saving the people.[4][King Genn Greymane](/wiki/Genn_Greymane) had heard that "druidism" was practiced among some of Gilneas's agrarian folk, but he was never personally exposed to it until he encountered night elf druids during the [Cataclysm](/wiki/Cataclysm_(event)).[6]

Harvest-witches who contracted the [worgen curse](/wiki/Worgen_curse) (which is druidic in origin) found that their powers were somewhat amplified. After making first contact with the night elves, cursed harvest witches were offered induction into the [Cenarion Circle](/wiki/Cenarion_Circle) for study and training.[3][Birch Tomlin](/wiki/Birch_Tomlin) refers to himself as a druid as well as being referred to as a harvest-witch, meaning the two terms have likely become synonymous.[7]

Like their countrymen, harvest-witches participate in the [Wickerman Festival](/wiki/Wickerman_Festival) on the Gilnean observance of [Hallow's End](/wiki/Hallow%27s_End). They believe that "as the woods sigh and shed their leaves", the people too must undergo a catharsis by throwing a branch onto the burning [wickerman](/wiki/Wickerman) to metaphorically relieve themselves of any emotional burdens they do not want to bear into winter.[8][9]

## Notes and trivia

- The harvest-witches seem to be thematically based on the real-world Celtic [druids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/druid) of ancient Britain (as opposed to the druids in the rest of the *Warcraft* franchise which have little in common with their real-world namesake). The fate of the old druidic religion of the harvest-witches being replaced by the worship of the Holy Light is similar to Celtic druidism's relationship with Christianity.

- [Seraphine of the Winter](/wiki/Seraphine_of_the_Winter) may be a harvest-witch, considering her concerns for trees and the similarity of her name with [Celestine of the Harvest](/wiki/Celestine_of_the_Harvest).
- [Marl Wormthorn](/wiki/Marl_Wormthorn) may also have been a harvest-witch, considering he is a worgen druid coming directly from Gilneas with refugees to the [Blasted Lands](/wiki/Blasted_Lands).
- Some of the [Kul Tiran](/wiki/Kul_Tiras_(kingdom)) humans who were taught druidism by the [Drust](/wiki/Drust)[Thornspeakers](/wiki/Thornspeakers) likely also had some influence from the harvest-witches, since the first humans on Kul Tiras came from Gilneas.
